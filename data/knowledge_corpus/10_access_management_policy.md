# TechNova Solutions — Password and Access Management Policy
## Purpose
This policy defines how employee and system access is requested, approved, reviewed, changed, and removed.

## Provisioning
Access requests must identify the employee, system, business justification, requested role, and appropriate approver. Access should be limited to the minimum necessary privileges.

## Manager Approval
The employee's manager is responsible for confirming that requested access is required for the employee's role. System owners may impose additional approval requirements.

## Privileged Access
Administrative or privileged access requires enhanced controls and should be granted only when necessary. Privileged accounts should not be used for routine activities when a standard account is sufficient.

## Role Changes
When an employee changes role, managers and system owners must review whether existing access remains necessary. Access no longer required should be removed.

## Termination
When employment ends, access must be disabled promptly according to the company's offboarding process. High-risk or privileged access may require immediate disablement.

## Authentication
Multi-factor authentication is required for systems designated by Security. Employees must protect authentication factors and report suspected compromise.

## Passwords
Passwords must be unique and must not be shared. Employees must use the approved password-management solution when one is provided.

## Reviews
System owners conduct periodic access reviews. Managers are expected to validate that access remains appropriate.

## Service Accounts
Service accounts must have an accountable owner, documented purpose, and appropriate credentials management. Credentials must not be hard-coded into source code or shared informally.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented risk acceptance and approval from the designated Security authority.

## Examples
A developer moving to a finance role should not automatically retain production engineering access. A departing employee's account should not remain active merely because a project manager has not yet found a replacement.

This policy complements the Information Security Policy and Employee Handbook.

## Access Requests
Requests should specify the system, role, business need, and duration when temporary access is required. Approvers should verify the need rather than approve access automatically.

## Temporary Access
Temporary or elevated access should have a defined purpose and, where supported, an expiration date. Employees should not retain privileged access after the business need ends.

## Joiner, Mover, Leaver Controls
Access management should cover the full employee lifecycle. New access is provisioned after approval, role changes trigger review, and departures trigger prompt disablement.

## Segregation of Duties
Where practical, critical activities should be separated so that one person cannot independently initiate and approve a sensitive transaction. System owners may define additional controls.

## Credential Protection
Authentication factors, recovery codes, and privileged credentials must be protected. Suspected credential compromise should be reported immediately through Security.

## Reviews and Evidence
Access reviews should document who reviewed the access, what was confirmed, and what changes were requested. Unresolved access-review exceptions should be escalated.

## Practical Guidance
Employees should request only the access needed for their work. Managers should challenge unnecessary privilege and promptly notify system owners when responsibilities change.

## Governance and Escalation
Employees should use the designated company system or process for formal requests, approvals, and records. Informal conversations can help coordinate work, but they do not replace required approval or documentation. When a situation does not clearly fit the policy, employees should pause before making a commitment and consult the responsible function such as HR, Finance, Procurement, IT, Security, Privacy, Legal, or their manager.

Managers are responsible for applying policies consistently, documenting material exceptions, and escalating unusual situations rather than creating undocumented local rules. Employees should provide enough context for the reviewing function to understand the business purpose, urgency, affected people or systems, and relevant risks.

Policies may be updated as business, legal, security, or operational requirements change. Employees should use the current approved version of the relevant policy when making a decision. Local law and employment agreements may impose additional requirements and should be followed where applicable.
