# TechNova Solutions — Procurement Policy
## Purpose
The Procurement Policy establishes controls for purchasing goods and services on behalf of TechNova.

## Purchase Planning
Employees should identify business need, estimated value, supplier requirements, and budget availability before committing company funds.

## Approval Thresholds
Purchases up to $1,000 may be approved by the employee's manager when budget is available. Purchases above $1,000 and up to $10,000 require manager and department budget-owner approval. Purchases above $10,000 require manager, budget-owner, and Procurement approval.

Purchases above $50,000 require Procurement review plus the additional approval specified in the applicable delegation-of-authority schedule.

## Competitive Sourcing
Procurement may require competitive quotations depending on spend, supplier risk, category, and contract terms. Employees must not split a purchase into smaller transactions to avoid an approval threshold.

## Supplier Onboarding
New suppliers must complete the applicable supplier onboarding and due-diligence process before purchase commitments are made. Required checks may include tax information, sanctions screening, security review, privacy review, and contract assessment depending on the supplier.

## Contracts
Employees should not sign supplier contracts unless they have delegated authority. Procurement and Legal should review agreements when required.

## Conflicts of Interest
Employees must disclose actual or potential conflicts of interest involving suppliers. Employees must not select suppliers for personal benefit.

## Emergency Purchases
Emergency purchases may follow an expedited process when delay would create material operational or safety risk. The reason and approvals must be documented.

## Purchase Orders
A purchase order or other approved purchasing mechanism should be established before a supplier begins work unless an approved exception applies.

## Examples
A $7,500 software purchase requires manager and budget-owner approval. A $15,000 supplier engagement requires Procurement approval in addition to the lower-level approvals. Splitting a $12,000 purchase into two $6,000 invoices to avoid Procurement approval is prohibited.

This policy should be read with Supplier Management, Information Security, Data Privacy, and the Code of Conduct.

## Budget Responsibility
Approval confirms that the purchaser has authority to commit funds; it does not replace the need for an available budget. Employees should verify budget availability before initiating a purchase.

## Competitive Quotes
When Procurement requests multiple quotations, employees should provide suppliers with consistent requirements so proposals can be compared fairly. Selection decisions should be based on legitimate business criteria.

## Purchase Splitting
The approval threshold applies to the underlying business commitment, not merely to individual invoices. Related purchases that are intentionally divided to avoid approval requirements may be treated as one commitment.

## Software and Technology
Technology purchases may require Security, Privacy, Architecture, Licensing, or IT review in addition to financial approval. Procurement approval alone does not authorize use of an unapproved technology product.

## Contract Changes
Material changes to price, scope, duration, data handling, or supplier risk may require reassessment and additional approval. Employees should not extend or materially modify supplier arrangements informally.

## Records
Purchase requests, approvals, quotations, contracts, and invoices should be retained according to company recordkeeping requirements.

## Practical Guidance
When the correct approval path is unclear, employees should contact Procurement before committing the company. Unauthorized commitments can create financial, legal, and operational risk.
