# TechNova Solutions — Travel and Expense Policy
## Purpose
This policy explains approval, booking, reimbursement, and documentation requirements for business travel and other eligible business expenses.

## Travel Approval
Business travel must receive manager approval before booking. International travel may require additional review for immigration, security, tax, or customer requirements.

## Booking
Employees should use approved corporate booking channels where available. Economy-class airfare is the standard for flights unless a documented exception applies. Reasonable hotel accommodation should be selected within company or location-specific limits.

## Ground Transportation
Reasonable business-related taxi, rideshare, rail, public transit, or rental-car expenses may be reimbursed when supported by documentation. Employees should select practical options consistent with business needs.

## Meals
Reasonable business-trip meals may be reimbursed within applicable location limits. Alcohol is not reimbursable under this policy. Personal meals unrelated to approved travel are not business expenses.

## Receipts
Employees must retain receipts or other required evidence for reimbursable expenses. Missing documentation may delay reimbursement or require an exception review.

## Submission
Expense reports should normally be submitted within 15 calendar days after returning from business travel. Managers are responsible for reviewing submitted expenses.

## Non-Reimbursable Items
Examples include personal entertainment, fines or penalties, upgrades for personal preference, expenses for family members unless specifically approved, and alcohol.

## Customer Expenses
Customer-related expenses require additional care. Employees must comply with customer rules and applicable anti-bribery requirements. Gifts, meals, or entertainment for customers must be modest, business-related, and permitted under applicable law and policy.

## Exceptions
Exceptions require documented approval from the appropriate authority. Employees should not assume that an expense will be reimbursed merely because it was incurred during a business trip.

## Examples
A train ticket for an approved business meeting is normally eligible when properly documented. A hotel room upgrade selected solely for personal preference is normally not eligible. A meal within the location limit may be reimbursed, but alcohol is not.

This policy should be used with the Code of Conduct and Procurement Policy where purchases are involved.

## Business Purpose
Every reimbursed expense should have a clear business purpose. Employees should record who attended when required, the business reason, date, and location for meals or customer-related activities.

## Currency and Conversion
Expenses incurred in a foreign currency should be reported using the approved expense-system conversion method or supported exchange-rate documentation where required. Employees should not intentionally select an exchange rate that increases reimbursement.

## Corporate Cards
Where a company card is provided, employees should use it for eligible business expenses according to card rules. Personal charges should be avoided and any accidental personal charge should be reported promptly.

## Advance Payments
Travel advances, where available, must be reconciled against actual eligible expenses. Unused amounts should be returned according to local finance procedures.

## Approval and Audit
Managers review expenses for business purpose, documentation, policy compliance, and reasonableness. Finance may conduct additional checks or request supporting evidence.

## Customer and Government Interactions
Expenses involving customers, public officials, or other external parties require special care. Employees must follow anti-bribery rules and any stricter customer requirements.

## Practical Guidance
When uncertain, employees should obtain approval before spending rather than assume reimbursement later. Good documentation reduces processing delays and makes the expense easier to audit.
