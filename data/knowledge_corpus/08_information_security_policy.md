# TechNova Solutions — Information Security Policy
## Purpose
This policy establishes baseline requirements for protecting TechNova information, systems, devices, and accounts.

## Access Control
Employees receive access based on job responsibilities and business need. Access should follow least-privilege principles. Shared accounts are prohibited unless a documented technical exception exists.

## Passwords and Authentication
Employees must use strong, unique passwords and approved multi-factor authentication where required. Passwords must never be shared through email, chat, or other informal channels.

## Phishing and Suspicious Activity
Employees should treat unexpected links, attachments, credential requests, or urgent payment requests as potentially suspicious. Suspected phishing should be reported through the approved security-reporting mechanism without interacting further with the suspicious content when practical.

## Devices
Company-managed devices must use approved security controls. Employees must install required security updates and should not disable endpoint protection.

## Data Protection
Confidential and restricted information must only be stored and transmitted using approved company systems. Sensitive information should not be copied to personal storage or unapproved cloud services.

## Remote Work
Remote employees must use approved secure access methods and take reasonable precautions against shoulder surfing, unauthorized screen viewing, and insecure networks.

## Incident Reporting
Loss of a company device, suspected credential compromise, malware, unauthorized access, or accidental disclosure must be reported promptly to the Security team. Employees should not attempt to conceal incidents.

## Software and AI Tools
Employees may use only software and AI tools approved for company data. Confidential information must not be pasted into public AI systems unless the tool and use case have been explicitly approved for that information classification.

## Access Reviews
Managers and system owners periodically review access. Access should be removed promptly when employees change roles or leave the company.

## Examples
An employee receiving a suspicious password-reset email should report it rather than clicking the link. An employee must not upload a confidential customer spreadsheet to a public AI service. A lost company laptop should be reported promptly even if it is password protected.

This policy works with Data Privacy, Acceptable Use, and Access Management requirements.

## Classification
Employees should understand the classification of information they handle. Public information may be shared more broadly, while confidential or restricted information requires stronger controls. When classification is unclear, employees should ask the information owner or Security.

## Email and Collaboration
Before sending information, employees should verify recipients and attachments. Auto-complete errors, incorrect mailing lists, and forwarding chains can cause accidental disclosure. Sensitive information should use approved secure-sharing mechanisms.

## Travel
Employees should avoid storing sensitive data locally on travel devices unless required. Devices should remain under the employee's control, and loss or theft should be reported immediately.

## Third-Party Services
External applications, browser extensions, storage services, and AI tools must be approved when they will access company information. Convenience is not a substitute for security review.

## Incident Response
Employees should preserve relevant evidence when practical and follow Security instructions after reporting an incident. They should not investigate beyond their role if doing so could destroy evidence or increase risk.

## Manager Responsibilities
Managers should ensure employees receive appropriate access and training and should support prompt access removal when responsibilities change.

## Practical Guidance
When unsure whether an action is safe, stop before sharing the information and consult Security. Early reporting is preferred over attempting to hide or independently resolve a potential incident.
