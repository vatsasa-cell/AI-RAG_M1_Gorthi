# TechNova Solutions — Performance Management Policy
## Purpose
The Performance Management Policy provides a consistent framework for setting expectations, reviewing performance, recognizing contributions, and addressing performance gaps.

## Goal Setting
At the beginning of the performance cycle, employees and managers agree on measurable goals aligned to team and company priorities. Goals should describe expected outcomes rather than only listing activities.

## Check-ins
Managers should conduct regular check-ins throughout the year. Check-ins are intended to discuss progress, priorities, blockers, development, and feedback. Employees may also request additional discussions when circumstances change.

## Annual Review
The annual review considers achievement of agreed goals, quality of work, collaboration, behaviors, customer impact, and development. Ratings should be supported by documented examples rather than isolated incidents.

## Development
Employees are encouraged to maintain a development plan. Managers should identify relevant training, mentoring, stretch assignments, or project opportunities.

## Performance Concerns
When performance is below expectations, the manager should communicate the concern clearly, provide examples, clarify expected improvement, and agree on reasonable next steps. Depending on severity and circumstances, HR may become involved.

## Improvement Plans
A formal performance improvement plan may be used when sustained improvement is required. The plan should define the performance gap, expected standard, support available, milestones, review dates, and consequences of insufficient improvement.

## Recognition
Strong performance should be recognized through appropriate feedback, awards, development opportunities, or other company programs.

## Fairness and Documentation
Managers should apply the process consistently and avoid decisions based on protected characteristics or other inappropriate factors. Relevant feedback and decisions should be documented.

## Appeals and Questions
Employees who disagree with a review should first discuss the matter with their manager. HR may be consulted where the issue remains unresolved or involves a policy concern.

## Examples
A missed deadline caused by a documented system outage should be assessed differently from repeated missed deadlines without escalation. A strong annual rating should reflect evidence across the review period rather than one successful project.

This policy should be read alongside the Employee Handbook and Code of Conduct.

## Evidence and Feedback
Managers should use specific examples when discussing performance. Evidence can include delivery against goals, quality measures, customer feedback, collaboration examples, and documented outcomes. Feedback should distinguish a one-time incident from a recurring pattern.

## Goal Changes
Business priorities can change during the year. When goals materially change, the manager and employee should update expectations rather than evaluating the employee against obsolete requirements.

## Support
Performance support may include coaching, training, mentoring, clearer priorities, additional resources, or changes to responsibilities. The appropriate support depends on the nature of the gap.

## Formal Process
A formal improvement plan should not be used as a surprise. Where such a plan is appropriate, the employee should understand the gap, expected standard, milestones, support, and review dates. HR may advise managers on the process.

## Documentation
Performance discussions and agreed actions should be documented in the designated HR or performance-management system. Documentation should be factual, professional, and focused on work-related matters.

## Calibration and Fairness
Where the organization uses review calibration, managers should be prepared to explain ratings using evidence. Employees should have an opportunity to discuss their review and clarify factual inaccuracies.

## Practical Guidance
The goal of performance management is not only to assign a rating. Regular communication, clear expectations, and timely support help employees understand what success looks like and how to achieve it.
