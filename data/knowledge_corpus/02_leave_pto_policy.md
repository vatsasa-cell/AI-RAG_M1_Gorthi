# TechNova Solutions — Leave and PTO Policy
## Purpose
This policy defines paid time off (PTO), approval expectations, carryover, and emergency absence procedures for eligible employees.

## Annual PTO Entitlement
Full-time employees receive 20 days of paid time off per calendar year. Employees joining during the year receive a prorated entitlement based on their eligible service period. Part-time employee entitlement is calculated according to the applicable employment arrangement.

PTO is intended for vacation, personal needs, rest, and other approved time away from work. Employees should plan leave early when possible, particularly around known business-critical periods.

## Request and Approval
Planned PTO must be requested through the HR system at least five business days before the first day of leave. The employee's manager approves or rejects the request based on staffing, customer commitments, and operational requirements.

Submitting a request does not itself constitute approval. Employees should wait for approval before making non-refundable travel arrangements.

## Carryover
Employees may carry forward up to five unused PTO days into the following calendar year. Carried-over days must normally be used by March 31 of the following year unless local law or a written employment agreement requires a different treatment.

Unused PTO above the permitted carryover limit generally expires at year-end, subject to local legal requirements.

## Sick and Emergency Absence
Unexpected illness or emergency absence should be reported to the manager as soon as reasonably possible. The employee should update the HR system when practical. Sick leave rules may vary by location and applicable law.

## Public Holidays
Company-observed public holidays are separate from PTO. The holiday calendar applicable to the employee's work location determines the observed holidays.

## Manager Responsibilities
Managers should review PTO requests consistently and consider workload, service coverage, and previously approved leave. Managers should not discourage employees from taking appropriate rest solely because of workload, although they may coordinate dates when legitimate business coverage requires it.

## Cancellation
Approved PTO may be changed or cancelled when business circumstances require, but managers should make reasonable efforts to avoid unnecessary disruption. Employees should communicate changes promptly.

## Policy Exceptions
Exceptions require HR approval unless a local law or employment agreement provides otherwise. Any exception should be documented in the HR system or another approved record.

## Examples
An employee with 20 annual PTO days who joins on July 1 will receive a prorated entitlement rather than the full annual amount. An employee with three unused days at year-end may carry all three into the next year. An employee with eight unused days may carry only five, subject to local rules.

This policy should be read together with the Employee Handbook and applicable local leave requirements.

## Planning Around Business Needs
Employees should review team calendars and known release, audit, customer, or operational periods before requesting extended leave. Managers should consider already approved absences and maintain appropriate service coverage. The objective is to coordinate dates, not to prevent employees from using entitled leave.

## Records and Audit Trail
The HR system is the authoritative record for PTO requests and approvals. Employees should not rely only on informal chat or email approval. If a manager approves an exception or change outside the normal workflow, the approval should be recorded through the designated HR process.

## Joining and Leaving Mid-Year
PTO entitlement for employees who join during the year is prorated. When an employee leaves the company, treatment of unused PTO follows applicable local law, employment terms, and company procedures. Employees should not assume that carryover or payout rules are identical across countries.

## Extended Leave
Long absences may require additional coordination with HR, payroll, benefits, and the employee's manager. Employees should provide required documentation where applicable and should confirm how the absence affects benefits or other employment arrangements.

## Practical Guidance
Employees should request leave early, wait for formal approval, and communicate changes promptly. Managers should apply the same approval principles to comparable situations and should escalate unusual cases to HR rather than creating undocumented arrangements.
