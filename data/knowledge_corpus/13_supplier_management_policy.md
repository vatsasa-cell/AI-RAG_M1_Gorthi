# TechNova Solutions — Supplier Management Policy
## Purpose
This policy establishes the lifecycle controls for selecting, onboarding, monitoring, and offboarding suppliers.

## Supplier Selection
Supplier selection should consider business capability, cost, quality, security, privacy, financial stability, compliance, and operational risk as appropriate to the service.

## Due Diligence
Before onboarding, suppliers may be subject to security, privacy, sanctions, financial, legal, insurance, and other checks based on risk. Higher-risk suppliers receive enhanced review.

## Contracts
Appropriate contractual terms should address services, confidentiality, security, data handling, service levels, audit rights, and termination where relevant.

## Risk Classification
Suppliers may be categorized by factors such as access to company systems, access to personal data, criticality of service, geographic exposure, and financial impact.

## Performance Monitoring
Business owners should monitor supplier performance against agreed service levels and quality expectations. Significant issues should be documented and escalated.

## Changes
Material changes in ownership, services, data processing, location, subcontractors, or security posture may trigger reassessment.

## Renewals
Supplier renewals should be reviewed before the existing agreement expires. Procurement, Legal, Security, Privacy, or other functions may need to participate depending on the supplier risk.

## Offboarding
When a supplier relationship ends, access should be removed, company assets returned, data handled according to contractual requirements, and outstanding obligations resolved.

## Procurement Relationship
Supplier onboarding does not replace procurement approval. Both processes may be required.

## Examples
A low-risk office-supply supplier may require a simpler review than a vendor processing employee personal data. A supplier that begins hosting data in a new country may require privacy and security reassessment.

This policy works with Procurement, Data Privacy, Information Security, and the Code of Conduct.

## Risk-Based Review
Not every supplier requires the same level of review. A vendor hosting sensitive data or connecting to production systems presents greater risk than a vendor supplying ordinary office materials.

## Security and Privacy
Security and Privacy reviews should consider what information the supplier can access, where it is processed, how it is protected, and whether subcontractors are involved.

## Business Ownership
Each important supplier should have a business owner responsible for the relationship, service performance, and escalation of material issues.

## Service Problems
Repeated missed service levels should be documented and discussed with the supplier. Material or unresolved issues may require escalation to Procurement, Legal, Security, or business leadership.

## Subcontractors
Changes to material subcontractors may trigger review depending on contract and risk. Business owners should ensure that required approvals are completed before accepting significant changes.

## Exit Planning
Supplier offboarding should include access removal, asset return, data return or deletion, contract closure, and transition planning where the service is business-critical.

## Practical Guidance
Supplier management is an ongoing lifecycle, not a one-time onboarding event. Risk should be reconsidered when services, data, ownership, geography, or security posture changes.

## Governance and Escalation
Employees should use the designated company system or process for formal requests, approvals, and records. Informal conversations can help coordinate work, but they do not replace required approval or documentation. When a situation does not clearly fit the policy, employees should pause before making a commitment and consult the responsible function such as HR, Finance, Procurement, IT, Security, Privacy, Legal, or their manager.

Managers are responsible for applying policies consistently, documenting material exceptions, and escalating unusual situations rather than creating undocumented local rules. Employees should provide enough context for the reviewing function to understand the business purpose, urgency, affected people or systems, and relevant risks.

Policies may be updated as business, legal, security, or operational requirements change. Employees should use the current approved version of the relevant policy when making a decision. Local law and employment agreements may impose additional requirements and should be followed where applicable.
